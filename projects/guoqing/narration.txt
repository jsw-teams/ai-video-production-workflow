A date on the national calendar can look like a single mark. But it can stand for very different beginnings.

In Taiwan, October tenth commemorates the Wuchang Uprising, the beginning of the 1911 Xinhai Revolution.

In the United States, July fourth is the day the Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence in 1776. The parchment signing began later, in August.

Singapore's August ninth marks the 1965 separation from Malaysia and independence as a republic.

Revolution. Declaration. Separation. These are not one historical template, and a public date does not give everyone the same day.

The public story sets the date. People decide what that date does in ordinary time.
