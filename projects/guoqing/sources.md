# Sources and factual boundaries

Checked 2026-09-29 against the linked official and archival pages. These sources support the three date explanations. The generated paintings are illustrative composites and are not used as evidence.

## The three dates

- **Taiwan / October 10:** The Office of the President describes the National Day known to the public as commemorating the Wuchang Uprising, which marked the beginning of the 1911 Xinhai Revolution. The film uses this narrow observance claim and does not make claims about competing sovereignty positions or treat October 10 as the date of a later constitutional act. [Office of the President, Republic of China (Taiwan), “National Day Celebrations”](https://www.president.gov.tw/qrcode/11e)
- **United States / July 4, 1776:** The U.S. National Archives states that the Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence on July 4. It also distinguishes the July 2 independence resolution and the parchment signing that began August 2. The film says “adopted,” not “signed.” [U.S. National Archives, Declaration of Independence](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/declaration-of-independence)
- **Singapore / August 9, 1965:** The Republic of Singapore Independence Act is dated August 9 and states that it provides for Singapore becoming an independent and sovereign republic separate from and independent of Malaysia. Parliament's historical account also dates the separation and independence to August 9, 1965. The film states this chronology without characterizing the separation as a simple voluntary choice by all residents. [Singapore Statutes Online, Republic of Singapore Independence Act 1965](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/RSIA1965), [Parliament of Singapore, Historical Development](https://www.parliament.gov.sg/history/historical-development)

## Interpretation

The connection among dates, transport, event setup, work, meals, celebration, and non-participation is the film's editorial observation. It is not presented as a survey result or a claim that everyone in a place responds alike. Generated images use anonymous composite figures and generic settings; they do not reconstruct named people or specific historical scenes.
